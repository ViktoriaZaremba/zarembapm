Why Estimates Fail

Most estimation problems don't start with "bad estimates" — they start much earlier, at the delivery process level.

1

Garbage in → Garbage out

If the team is estimating:
• unclear scope
• tasks without acceptance criteria
• features with unknown dependencies

That's why Definition of Ready directly impacts estimation quality.

The estimate becomes guessing, not forecasting.
2

"Done" means different things

For some, a task ends after coding.
For others — after QA, release, or stabilization.

Without a shared Definition of Done, the team estimates different amounts of work — even when talking about the same task.
3

Estimate ≠ Timeline

Estimate is effort.
Timeline is effort + meetings + bugs + testing + dependencies + operational overhead.

That's why "5 days estimate" almost never means "5 calendar days delivery".
4

100% capacity doesn't exist

Teams have ceremonies, support, syncs, bugs, context switching, and other systemic load.

So focus factor isn't an "excuse" — it's a real part of the planning process.

Key Takeaway

Accurate estimates require accurate systems.

As long as the delivery process is chaotic — even the strongest team won't be able to consistently provide accurate forecasts.
